Why 1400 × 900

  • owner-decision 2026-10-31
    Arithmetic, not taste. The renderer draws text at 16px (bootstrap-tools TEXT_STYLE; owner, 2026-10-31: "maybe make tasks a bit taller and font a bit bigger?"). A page shows a diagram at most about 1200px wide once the nav rail is taken off a 1440px screen, so a diagram W wide shows its text at 16 × 1200 / W px: about 14px at 1400. About 760px of a 900px screen is left under the header and the figure toolbar, so 900 keeps a diagram on about one screen. Change the budget and the text size together.

The procedure

  • pr 2026-11-01 2026-10-31
    1. Run the check before you edit, and note the counts. 2. Fix the lane order first. --write tries every order of the lanes (up to 8) and keeps the one whose flows cross the fewest lane boundaries, only if it adds no flow crossing and draws no flow through a box. It moves shapes with their lane, and each flow between lanes keeps its bends, each moving with the lane it lies in; only a flow that would then run through a box is re-drawn, by the first right-angled route that runs through none. If the layout fixes together would add a flow through a box, a crossing or a slant, the file keeps its layout and only its labels move. Look at the result: it is a start, not a layout. 3. Make it compact. Tasks a fixed 120 × 120 square (owner, 2026-11-01: "FIXED square"; --write squares them), gateways 50, events 36. A name that does not fit at 16px is shortened, never given a bigger box (text-overflow). About 40–70 between neighbours in a row; wider only where a flow label needs it. A column can hold one step per lane: a hand-off straight down from one lane to the next is the shortest flow there is. A lane is as tall as its rows plus room for labels, no more. 4. Right angles only (owner, 2026-10-31, on dth-pc: "straight angled arrows ugly"). A flow between lanes leaves the side facing its target, turns once in the gap between the two columns, and enters the target's side: never one slanted line from box to box. 5. Route the loops below. A loop-back leaves through the bottom, runs in the channel under the row and comes up into its target ([bpmn-processes](bpmn-processes.md) §"Edge routing"). A flow between lanes leaves on the side facing its target. 6. Place every label. A gateway's question goes above the diamond, its answers beside their flows; an event's name beside or below it, clear of the flows leaving it and of the lane's name strip; a flow label beside its own segment; and every label inside one lane, never across a lane line. --write moves each label that touches a flow, a shape, another label, a lane line or a name strip to the nearest clear place (around its shape, or beside a run of its flow), and writes that as its BPMNLabel bounds; what it cannot place it still reports. Give a multi-line label room for the lines it wraps to: bpmn-js wraps an outside label to the width of its BPMNLabel bounds, so a gateway question about 40 characters long is five lines (about 90 tall) at width 90 and three lines at width 130. The check measures the text bpmn-js drew, not the bounds. 7. Run the check again: zero findings on the diagram you touched, or say in the PR which remain and why. 8. Look at the rendered SVG, at 100% in a 1200px band. The check finds collisions; only looking finds a diagram that is clean and still confusing.
